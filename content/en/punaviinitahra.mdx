The glass tips over and a dark patch spreads across your light fabric sofa. The instinct is to scrub hard and pour salt on it — exactly how most red wine stains become permanent. The good news: if you stay calm and work methodically in the first few minutes, you have a real chance of removing the stain completely.

Below you will find what to do straight away, which household tricks are myths, and when it makes sense to let a professional take over.

## Why red wine sticks to fabric so easily

Red wine gets its colour from grape-skin pigments called anthocyanins, together with tannins. They are natural dyes and behave much like plant dye on fibres. While the wine is still wet, the pigment is loose and comes out fairly easily. As it dries, it bonds to the fibre. Heat, harsh chemicals and rubbing all speed that up.

So the goal of the first minutes is simple: get as much liquid out of the fabric as possible before it soaks into the cushion and dries.

## First aid, step by step

1. **Blot immediately.** Use a clean, white, absorbent cloth or paper towels and press them onto the stain. Move to a dry area of the cloth as soon as it picks up colour. Do not rub.
2. **Work from the edges inwards.** This keeps the stain from spreading.
3. **Add a little cold water.** Dab a small amount of cold water onto the stain and blot again. Repeat a few times. Don't pour: a soaked cushion tends to leave a water ring as it dries.
4. **Mild detergent if needed.** Mix a drop of clear washing-up liquid into a glass of cold water. Dab it on, let it work for a minute or two, then blot. Finish by dabbing with plain water so no detergent stays in the fabric.
5. **Let it dry.** Press a dry towel on the area and leave it to dry at room temperature. Skip the hair dryer.

<Callout type="tip" title="Test on a hidden spot first">
Try any solution on an inconspicuous area first, such as under a seat cushion or on the back of the sofa. Wait a moment and check that the fabric doesn't fade and the colour doesn't bleed.
</Callout>

## What not to do

| Household trick | Why it backfires |
|---|---|
| Pouring salt on it | Salt soaks up liquid but can dry the pigment into the fibres. Dried salt is also hard to get out of upholstery. |
| Pouring white wine on it | Adds more liquid and sugar, so the stain spreads. |
| Hot water | Heat sets the dye. |
| Scrubbing | Pushes colour deeper and can fuzz or damage the fabric. |
| Chlorine bleach | Can damage the fibres and leave a permanent pale patch. |
| Mixing bleach with vinegar or ammonia | Never do this — it can release dangerous gases. |

Laundry stain removers are often too strong for upholstery and may take some colour with the stain.

## Check the care label and the material

Before applying anything beyond blotting, look for the care label. "W" means water-based cleaning is fine, "S" means solvent-based cleaning only, and "X" means vacuum only. If the label says "S" or "X", avoid water altogether and leave the stain to a professional.

Take extra care with:

- **Genuine suede and nubuck** — water marks them easily; they are not wet-cleaned.
- **Linen and viscose** — high risk of shrinkage and water rings.
- **Silk** — not a candidate for home remedies.
- **Leather** — follows its own rules; see [leather sofa cleaning](/en/leather-sofa-cleaning).

## If the stain has already dried

A dried wine stain is harder, but not always a lost cause. Lightly dampen it with cold water and give it a few minutes to soften. Then continue as with a fresh stain: mild solution, dabbing, blotting and rinsing with clean water.

If the stain doesn't fade after two or three rounds, stop. Repeated soaking and rubbing at home often leaves a ring around the stain that ends up more visible than the stain itself.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't soak the cushion">
Too much water runs through the fabric into the foam. As the filling dries, it wicks dirt and dye back up to the surface and leaves a ring. Little and often is always safer.
</Callout>

## When to call a professional

Home methods work best on small, fresh stains. Professional upholstery cleaning at home is worth considering if:

- the stain is large or the wine has soaked through the cushion
- the stain has dried or has already been treated several times
- the fabric is light, delicate, or you're unsure how it should be cleaned
- a water ring has already formed.

## Prevention: fabric protection

If wine, coffee or juice is a regular feature on your sofa, fabric protection can be worth considering once the fabric is clean and completely dry. It doesn't make the fabric stain-proof, but it slows down how quickly liquid soaks in, which buys you a few valuable seconds to blot.

## In short

- Blot right away with a clean white cloth — never rub.
- Use cold water and, if needed, a mild washing-up liquid solution; rinse it out afterwards.
- Forget salt, hot water and bleach.
- Check the care label before wetting the fabric.
- Large, dried stains or delicate fabrics are best left to a professional before home experiments make the stain permanent.
