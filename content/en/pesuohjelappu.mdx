Before you put any cleaner on your sofa, look for the care label. Upholstered furniture often carries a single letter or letter pair that tells you how the fabric may be cleaned. It is a small detail, but it is what decides whether a stain comes out or a water ring stays behind.

The codes come from the furniture industry and are most common on American and international brands. Not every sofa has one, but if yours does, it is the best place to start.

## Where to find the label

- under the seat cushions, on the frame
- inside a removable cover, near the zip
- on the underside or the lower back edge of the sofa
- in the side seam of a loose cushion.

Sometimes the label has text instead of a code, such as "Clean with water-based shampoo" or "Dry clean only". Same message. If the label has been cut off, check the receipt, the manufacturer's website or search by the fabric name.

## The codes at a glance

| Code | Meaning | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| W | Water-based cleaners | Can be cleaned with water and water-based upholstery shampoos. Suitable for steam and extraction cleaning. |
| S | Solvent-based cleaners only | Water may cause rings, shrinkage or fading. Dry or solvent methods only. |
| WS or SW | Water or solvent | Either type of cleaner is fine. Wet cleaning usually works as long as the fabric is not over-wetted. |
| X | Vacuum only | No water and no solvents. Vacuuming and light brushing only. |

### W: the easy one

W fabrics are usually polyester, microfibre or blends. They handle hot water extraction well, and most everyday stains come out. Even so, too much water is not good: a soaked filling dries slowly.

### S: keep water away

An S code means water is a risk. Typical examples are some natural fibres, viscose and certain velvets or specially finished fabrics. Scrubbing an S fabric with a wet sponge easily leaves a ring, shrinks it or flattens the pile for good.

### WS: flexible, not carefree

A professional picks the method based on the stain. Moisture is still kept moderate.

### X: vacuum only

X fabrics are rare. Nothing liquid goes on them, and stains on X fabric are often permanent.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not test in the middle of the seat">
If you are unsure of the code, never experiment on a visible area. Test on a hidden spot and wait until it is fully dry before drawing conclusions.
</Callout>

## What to do if there is no label

This is common with older or second-hand sofas.

1. **Find out the material** by searching the manufacturer and model name.
2. **Do a small test.** Dampen a white cloth with water and press it gently on a hidden area. If colour transfers to the cloth or a ring appears once dry, water is not suitable.
3. **Avoid strong products** until you know what the fabric tolerates.
4. **Ask a professional.** A photo often tells a lot.

## Common mistakes

- ignoring the label and going straight for a rental machine
- using too much water, even on a W fabric
- rubbing a stain instead of blotting it
- cleaning just the stain, which often leaves a visible edge
- using household detergents that leave foam and residue.

## In short

- Look for the label under cushions, on the underside and inside zipped covers.
- W means water-based, S solvent only, WS either, and X vacuum only.
- No label? Test on a hidden spot and skip strong products.
- S and X fabrics, as well as suede, nubuck and silk, are not for standard wet cleaning.
