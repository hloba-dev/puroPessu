A well-kept leather sofa can last for decades, yet the wrong cleaner can damage it in a single evening. Most leather damage does not come from wear and tear but from well-meant care: baby wipes, washing-up liquid or a cloth that is far too wet.

Good leather sofa care is not complicated. The first step is finding out what kind of leather you actually have, because different types react very differently to water, chemicals and sunlight.

## The main types of upholstery leather

| Type | How to recognise it | What it means for care |
|---|---|---|
| Aniline | Soft, natural look, visible pores and marks, no protective coating | Absorbs liquids and body oils easily, fades in sunlight |
| Semi-aniline | Thin pigment or protective layer, still fairly natural surface | A little more durable than aniline but still delicate |
| Pigmented (protected) | Even colour, pigment and topcoat on the surface | The most common and easiest to live with, tolerates a lightly damp wipe |
| Faux leather (PU, PVC) | Perfectly uniform surface, repeating grain, no leather smell | Needs no conditioning, but can crack from heat and harsh products |

### A quick test at home

Pick a hidden spot, such as under a seat cushion or the back of the sofa. Place one small drop of water on the leather and watch it for about thirty seconds.

- **The drop soaks in and darkens the spot:** most likely aniline.
- **It soaks in slowly:** probably semi-aniline.
- **It beads on the surface:** pigmented or faux leather.

Blot the drop away straight after with a soft dry cloth. Faux leather often gives itself away with a fabric backing visible near a seam.

## Everyday care: less is more

1. **Vacuum** the seams and gaps between cushions with a soft brush attachment a couple of times a month. Grit and crumbs act like sandpaper.
2. **Dust** with a dry or well-wrung microfibre cloth. Use only a dry cloth on aniline.
3. **Blot spills straight away** and do not rub. Rubbing spreads the liquid and wears the surface.
4. **Clean properly** with a dedicated leather cleaner a few times a year, either yourself or with a professional.

<Callout type="tip" title="One cloth, one job">
Keep a dedicated light-coloured microfibre cloth for the leather sofa. A coloured cloth can stain pale leather, and kitchen cloths usually carry grease and detergent residue.
</Callout>

## What ruins a leather sofa

- **Baby wipes and wet wipes.** They often contain alcohol, preservatives and surfactants that dry out the leather and slowly wear through the pigment layer.
- **Alcohol and disinfectants** strip colour and dry leather fast, especially aniline.
- **Washing-up liquid and all-purpose cleaners** are designed to dissolve grease, and leather's natural oils are exactly that. The result is dry, stiff leather.
- **Steam on aniline leather** can leave permanent dark patches and change the feel. Steam cleaning is for fabric sofas, not delicate leather.
- **Direct sunlight** fades leather quickly and unevenly, aniline most of all.
- **Radiators and underfloor heating** dry leather until it cracks. Keep the sofa at least half a metre from a heat source if you can.

<Callout type="warning" title="Skip the home remedies">
Vinegar, olive oil, toothpaste and shoe polish do not belong on a sofa. Oil can go rancid and leave a dark stain that will not come out.
</Callout>

## Conditioning and protection

Leather is skin, and it needs some moisture to stay supple.

- **Pigmented leather:** a conditioner or protector once or twice a year is usually enough.
- **Aniline and semi-aniline:** use only products made for these leathers and always test on a hidden area first. The wrong product can darken them permanently.
- **Faux leather:** no conditioning needed, just a damp cloth and a mild cleaner suitable for it.

Apply conditioner thinly and only on clean leather. Conditioning over dirt seals the dirt in, so the order is always clean first, condition second.

## When to call a professional

- the armrests and headrests have turned dark and shiny with body oils
- there is ink, jeans dye or another colour transfer
- you are not sure whether your leather is aniline or pigmented
- the sofa smells of smoke or pets
- the leather needs a thorough clean before you start conditioning it yourself.

## In short

- Identify the leather type with a water-drop test on a hidden spot.
- Vacuum and dust regularly, and blot spills rather than rubbing.
- No baby wipes, alcohol, washing-up liquid, or steam on aniline.
- Keep the sofa out of direct sun and away from radiators.
- Clean before you condition, and choose products for your leather type.
- Darkened armrests and dye transfer are best handled by a professional before they set in.
