Your nose gets used to the smells of your own home quickly, so a dog smell in the sofa is usually pointed out by a visitor — or noticed when you come back from a trip. For a dog, the sofa is the best spot in the house: soft, warm and smelling of you. Over time, though, the fabric and filling collect plenty of things that smell.

Here's where the odour actually comes from, what genuinely helps at home, and why it so often returns even after the sofa has smelled fresh for a while.

## Where the smell really comes from

It's rarely the hair alone. Usually the smell is the sum of several things:

- **Skin oils.** A dog's skin produces sebum that protects the coat. It transfers to the fabric, and the bacteria living in it create that familiar "doggy" smell.
- **Dander and hair.** They don't smell much on their own, but they hold oil and moisture and feed bacteria.
- **Moisture.** A wet dog after a rainy walk dampens the upholstery, and damp oils smell much stronger.
- **Saliva and dirty paws.** Sand, mud and damp come straight in from outside.
- **Urine.** A puppy, an older dog or a stressed one may mark or have an accident. Urine soaks right into the filling and is by far the hardest odour to deal with.

The key point: much of the source isn't on the surface of the fabric but inside the fibres and the cushion filling.

## What actually helps at home

1. **Vacuum regularly.** Use the upholstery nozzle and go over seams, under cushions and the gap by the backrest. Once a week is a good rhythm in a dog household.
2. **Cover the dog's spot.** A washable throw or cover is far easier to clean than the whole sofa. Wash it often.
3. **Dry the dog before the sofa.** A towel by the door noticeably cuts down on moisture and paw dirt.
4. **Air the room.** Fresh air reduces humidity, though it won't remove oils from the fabric.
5. **Deal with accidents straight away.** Blot urine or drool immediately and, if needed, use an enzyme cleaner made for pet stains — tested on a hidden spot first.

<Callout type="tip" title="Use an enzyme cleaner for urine">
For urine, choose an enzyme-based pet stain remover. Enzymes break down the compounds in urine, whereas ordinary detergent mostly just dilutes them. Avoid ammonia-based products: their smell can encourage the dog to mark the same spot again.
</Callout>

## What only masks the smell

| Method | What it really does |
|---|---|
| Baking soda | Absorbs a little odour from the surface but doesn't remove oils or reach the filling. Vacuum the residue out thoroughly. |
| Fabric fresheners | Cover the smell for a while. Once the scent fades, the dog smell is back. |
| Vinegar solution | May help with a light surface odour, but too much moisture reaches the filling and can make things worse. |
| Handheld steamer | Heats the surface but doesn't rinse or extract any dirt. The moisture stays in the fabric. |

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't mix chemicals">
Never mix chlorine bleach with vinegar or ammonia — the combination can release dangerous gases. Bleach can also permanently damage upholstery fabric.
</Callout>

## Why the smell comes back after a few days

This is the question dog owners ask most. The surface gets cleaned or sprayed fresh, and a few days later the smell is back. There are three reasons:

- **The source is in the filling.** Surface treatment doesn't reach the oils, dander and urine that have worked their way deep inside.
- **Moisture reactivates it.** When humidity rises, someone sits down or a warm dog lies there, the old smell resurfaces.
- **Detergent residue.** Household cleaner left in the fabric attracts new dirt and oil, and the cycle continues.

Lasting odour removal means rinsing the dirt, oils and odour-causing compounds out from inside the fibres, not just the surface.

## How often to clean a sofa in a dog household

With a dog, a sofa gets dirty faster. A reasonable rule of thumb is professional cleaning about twice a year, or more often if the dog is large, sheds heavily or the fabric is light. Regular vacuuming and a washable cover stretch the interval considerably.

## In short

- Dog smell comes from oils, dander, moisture and bacteria, not just hair.
- Vacuuming, a washable cover and a towel by the door keep it under control.
- Baking soda and fresheners mask the smell but don't remove its source.
- The smell returns because the source is in the filling and inside the fibres.
- A lasting result needs steam cleaning with extraction that rinses dirt out from deep down.
