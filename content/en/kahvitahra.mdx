Morning coffee on the sofa, laptop on your knees, one clumsy elbow — and there's a brown patch on the upholstery. Coffee is one of the most common stains on fabric sofas, often several layered in the same favourite spot. A fresh coffee stain usually comes out without much trouble. Hot water and hard scrubbing, however, can turn it into a stubborn one.

Two things decide how you should treat it: whether the coffee had milk or sugar in it, and how long the stain has been sitting.

## What a coffee stain is made of

Black coffee contains brown pigments and tannins that dye fibres much like tea or red wine. Milk adds protein and fat. Sugar and syrups leave a sticky residue that quickly attracts dust and dirt.

| Stain type | What makes it tricky | First aid |
|---|---|---|
| Black coffee | Pigments and tannins | Blot, cold or lukewarm water, mild detergent |
| Milky coffee, latte, cappuccino | Protein and fat as well as colour | Cold water only, washing-up liquid for the fat |
| Sweetened coffee | Sugar stays sticky and collects dirt | Thorough rinsing with clean water |
| Dried coffee stain | Colour has set, often with a ring | Soften with cold water, be patient |

## Fresh coffee stain: what to do right away

1. **Blot.** Press a clean white cloth or paper towel onto the stain. Switch to a dry area as soon as it picks up colour. Don't rub — that pushes the colour deeper and spreads it.
2. **Cold water, a little at a time.** Dab on some cold water and blot straight away. Repeat until the cloth no longer turns brown.
3. **Mild solution.** Mix a drop of clear washing-up liquid into a glass of cold water. Dab it on, leave it for a couple of minutes, then blot.
4. **Rinse.** Dab with clean water and blot, so no detergent or sugar is left behind. Leftover detergent attracts new dirt.
5. **Dry.** Press with a dry towel and let it air-dry at room temperature.

<Callout type="tip" title="Edges first, then the centre">
Start at the outer edge of the stain and work inwards. That keeps it from spreading and reduces the chance of a ring.
</Callout>

## Milky coffee needs cold water

If there was milk in the coffee, hot water is especially unhelpful: heat makes milk proteins bond to the fibres and sets the stain. Stick to cold water from start to finish.

Ordinary washing-up liquid is good at lifting milk fat — that's what it's made for. Rinse carefully afterwards. Milk left in the upholstery, particularly if it reached the cushion filling, can start to smell over time.

## Dried or old coffee stains

Coffee stains are often noticed later, as a brown ring. Try this:

- Lightly dampen the stain with cold water and let it soften for a few minutes.
- Dab on the washing-up liquid solution, let it work and blot.
- If colour remains, many fabrics tolerate a weak vinegar solution (about one part regular white vinegar to two parts water). Test it on a hidden spot first and rinse with clean water afterwards.
- Stop if the stain doesn't fade after two or three rounds.

<Callout type="warning" title="Never mix bleach with vinegar">
Never combine chlorine-based products with vinegar or ammonia — the mixture can release toxic gases. Avoid chlorine bleach on upholstery fabric altogether; it easily leaves a permanent pale patch.
</Callout>

## Why a ring appears around the stain

Plenty of people find that the stain disappears but a ring takes its place. The usual cause is too much water. When the fabric gets soaked, water reaches the filling and dissolves old dirt there. As it dries, moisture evaporates from the surface and the dirt collects at the edge of the wet area.

That's why water should go on sparingly, with thorough blotting after each round. Once a ring has formed, it can usually only be evened out by cleaning the whole seat or cushion uniformly, with equipment that extracts the water and dirt at the same time.

## Check the material before you start

Look at the care label before wetting anything. "S" or "X" means the fabric can't take water-based cleaning, so don't experiment. Genuine suede, nubuck and silk are not wet-cleaned; linen and viscose are prone to shrinking and water marks. On a leather sofa, wipe the coffee off immediately with a damp cloth and follow up with a leather care product — see [leather sofa cleaning](/en/leather-sofa-cleaning).

## In short

- Blot immediately — don't rub.
- Use cold water, especially if the coffee had milk in it.
- A mild washing-up liquid solution lifts colour and fat; always rinse afterwards.
- Apply water sparingly to avoid rings.
