Nobody likes the idea of tiny creatures living in their mattress. But dust mites are a normal part of every home, and finding them says nothing about your hygiene. For most people they're harmless. For people with a dust mite allergy or asthma, though, they can cause symptoms and disrupt sleep — right where we spend a third of our lives.

Here's what you should know about dust mites and which measures genuinely reduce their numbers in a mattress.

## What dust mites are

Dust mites are arachnids about a third of a millimetre long, too small to see with the naked eye. They don't bite and don't spread disease. They feed on the skin flakes we shed constantly, which collect above all in bedding, mattresses and pillows.

Mites thrive when there is:

- warmth, around room temperature
- high humidity
- plenty of food, meaning skin flakes.

A bed ticks every box. While we sleep we warm the mattress, release moisture through sweat and leave skin flakes behind. That makes the mattress about the best habitat in the house.

## Why dust mites cause symptoms

It's not the mites themselves that trigger allergies, but their droppings and the fragments of dead mites. These contain proteins that set off an allergic reaction in sensitised people. As the particles dry and break down, they get stirred into the air with dust — when you make the bed, for example.

Typical symptoms include a blocked nose in the morning, sneezing, itchy eyes and a night-time cough, often worse in bed and easing during the day. If you suspect a dust mite allergy, see a doctor: it's diagnosed with tests, not from symptoms alone.

<Callout type="tip" title="The Finnish winter is on your side">
Indoor air in Finnish homes is usually dry in winter, and mites don't like dry conditions. Keep the bedroom cool and well ventilated in summer too, when humidity rises.
</Callout>

## What helps day to day

You can't get rid of dust mites completely, but you can noticeably reduce their numbers and the build-up of allergens.

### Wash bedding at 60 °C

A 60 °C wash kills dust mites and rinses out allergens. Wash sheets and pillowcases about once a week. Check the care labels on pillows and duvets: many can take 60 °C and are worth washing a few times a year. If an item can't handle a hot wash, 24 hours in the freezer kills the mites, but you still need to wash the allergens out.

### Air the bed and the bedroom

Don't make the bed as soon as you get up. Pull the duvet back so the mattress can breathe and the moisture from the night can evaporate. Ventilate the bedroom regularly. In winter, you can do as the Finns do and air duvets, pillows and the mattress topper outside in the frost.

### Use a mattress protector

A washable mattress protector keeps sweat and skin flakes from going straight into the mattress, and it can be washed regularly. For allergy sufferers there are encasings made from tightly woven fabric that mites and allergens can't pass through, zipped around the whole mattress.

### Vacuum and reduce dust

Vacuum the mattress now and then with the upholstery nozzle, ideally with a HEPA-filter vacuum. Cut down on dust-collecting textiles in the bedroom, such as extra cushions and soft toys.

## What about the mattress itself

Bedding goes in the washing machine; a mattress doesn't. Many people try to clean a mattress at home by soaking or scrubbing it, and that's a bad idea.

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't soak your mattress">
Mattress filling absorbs water readily but dries very slowly. A damp mattress is an ideal breeding ground for mould and bacteria — and it attracts dust mites, too. Keep water to a minimum and make sure the mattress dries completely.
</Callout>

## How often to clean a mattress

| Situation | Suggested interval for professional cleaning |
|---|---|
| Typical household | About once a year |
| Dust mite allergy or asthma in the family | About twice a year |
| A pet sleeps in the bed | About twice a year |
| Child's mattress with occasional accidents | As needed, stains straight away |

Weekly bedding washes and a mattress protector are the foundation; regular mattress cleaning builds on top of them.

## In short

- Dust mites live in almost every bed; it's not a hygiene failure.
- Symptoms come from their droppings and remains, not the mites themselves.
- Wash bedding weekly at 60 °C, air the bed and use a mattress protector.
- Don't soak a mattress at home.
- Professional steam cleaning with extraction kills mites and removes allergens from inside the mattress — about once or twice a year is a good rhythm.
