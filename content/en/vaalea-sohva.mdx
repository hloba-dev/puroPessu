A light-coloured sofa makes a living room feel bright and calm, but it shows everything. A coffee splash, the blue shadow from jeans or armrests that slowly darken stand out far more on pale fabric than on dark.

The good news is that a light sofa does not get any dirtier than a dark one. The dirt is just visible. With the right habits, it stays looking good for a long time.

## Everyday care

- **Vacuum once a week** with a soft brush attachment, including the seams and gaps between cushions.
- **Flip and rotate cushions** if they are loose, so wear and yellowing spread evenly.
- **Plump the cushions** so the fabric does not compress and darken in the same spots.
- **Keep it out of direct sun.** Some light fabrics yellow or fade unevenly.
- **Use a throw** where the dog or cat likes to lie.

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep a white cloth handy">
Keep a clean white microfibre or cotton cloth nearby. When something spills, blot straight away. A white cloth also shows you whether the stain is lifting or the fabric colour is starting to run.
</Callout>

## When something spills

1. **Act quickly** but calmly.
2. **Blot, do not rub.** Rubbing spreads the stain and roughens the fibres, so the area looks cloudy once dry.
3. **Work from the edges towards the centre** so the stain does not spread.
4. **Use as little water as possible.** Too much moisture spreads the stain and can leave a ring.
5. **Check the care label** before using any product: W and WS fabrics tolerate water, S and X do not.

## Jeans dye on a light sofa

New dark jeans are a light sofa's worst enemy. Indigo transfers to the fabric through friction and moisture, typically as a bluish shadow on the front edge of the seat and on the backrest.

It is harder to remove than ordinary dirt because it is dye, not surface soil. Fresh transfer usually comes out well with professional cleaning, but old, built-up dye may end up lighter but still visible.

Prevention is easiest:

- wash new dark jeans a couple of times before sitting on a light sofa
- put a throw on your usual seat while the jeans are brand new
- deal with blue shadows early, before they set.

## Why armrests and headrests turn grey

Armrests, the front edge of the seat and headrest areas go grey even without a single real stain. The cause is skin oil, sweat, hair products and hand cream. The oil binds dust, and the fabric darkens so gradually that you only notice when you compare it with the back of the sofa.

This kind of oily soil does not come off with a vacuum or a damp cloth. It needs a degreasing pre-treatment followed by rinsing, in other words a proper clean.

## What to avoid on a light sofa

- **No bleach or chlorine.** They can leave yellow patches and weaken the fibres.
- **No coloured cloths or sponges.** Dye can transfer from a damp cloth.
- **No scrubbing** with a brush or a rough sponge.
- **No stain pens or sprays without testing.** Many leave a pale or yellowish ring.
- **No hairdryer.** Hot air can set the stain and leave an edge.

<Callout type="warning" title="Spot cleaning leaves a border">
When you clean only the stain, dirt and dissolved dye collect at the line between wet and dry fabric. Once dry, that line often shows more than the original stain, especially on pale fabric. That is why a surface should always be cleaned from seam to seam.
</Callout>

## In short

- Vacuum weekly, rotate cushions and keep the sofa out of direct sun.
- Blot spills straight away with a white cloth, edges to centre, no rubbing.
- Wash new jeans before sitting on a light sofa.
- Grey armrests are oily soil that needs a proper clean.
- Avoid bleach, coloured cloths and cleaning only the stain.
- Regular seam-to-seam cleaning keeps a light sofa light for longest.
